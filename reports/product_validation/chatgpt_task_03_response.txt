The immediate surrounding passage suggests something more active than simple passivity. In this source’s translation, hukam rajāī chalṇā is rendered as walking in accordance with Hukam, and the question it answers is specifically how one becomes truthful and breaks through illusion.

1. The line in its immediate context — Shabad DMP, Ang 1

Guru Nanak first lists several things that do not solve the problem:

ਸੋਚੈ. ਸੋਚਿ ਨ ਹੋਵਈ; ਜੇ ਸੋਚੀ ਲਖ ਵਾਰ ॥
sochai. soch na hovee; je sochee lakh vaar

ਚੁਪੈ. ਚੁਪ ਨ ਹੋਵਈ; ਜੇ ਲਾਇ ਰਹਾ ਲਿਵ ਤਾਰ ॥
chupai. chup na hovee; je laae rahaa liv taar

ਭੁਖਿਆ. ਭੁਖ ਨ ਉਤਰੀ; ਜੇ ਬੰਨਾ ਪੁਰੀਆ ਭਾਰ ॥
bhukhiaa. bhukh na utaree; je banaa pureea bhaar

ਸਹਸ ਸਿਆਣਪਾ. ਲਖ ਹੋਹਿ; ਤ ਇਕ. ਨ ਚਲੈ ਨਾਲਿ ॥
sehas siaanapaa. lakh hohi; ta ik. na chalai naal

English translation in the source:

“By thinking, He cannot be reduced to thought, even by thinking hundreds of thousands of times.”

“By remaining silent, inner silence is not obtained, even by remaining lovingly absorbed deep within.”

“The hunger of the hungry is not appeased, even by piling up loads of worldly goods.”

“Hundreds of thousands of clever tricks, but not even one of them will go along with you in the end.” sggs_multilingual.txt

Then comes the question:

ਕਿਵ. ਸਚਿਆਰਾ ਹੋਈਐ; ਕਿਵ. ਕੂੜੈ ਤੁਟੈ ਪਾਲਿ ॥
kiv. sachiaaraa hoeeai; kiv. koorrai tuttai paal

Source translation:

“So how can you become truthful? And how can the veil of illusion be torn away?” sggs_multilingual.txt

And the answer:

ਹੁਕਮਿ ਰਜਾਈ ਚਲਣਾ; ਨਾਨਕ. ਲਿਖਿਆ ਨਾਲਿ ॥੧॥
hukam rajaaee chalanaa; naanak. likhiaa naal

Source translation:

“O Nanak, it is written that you shall obey the Hukam of His Command, and walk in the Way of His Will.” sggs_multilingual.txt

My interpretation: The operative word here is ਚਲਣਾ (chalṇā) — “to walk/go.” Even without imposing a different translation on it, the supplied English itself says “walk in the Way”, not merely “accept whatever happens.”

The structure is essentially:

How do I become truthful? How does illusion break? → Walk according to Hukam.

So within this passage, Hukam is being presented as a way of living, not merely a psychological attitude toward events after they occur.

2. The very next passage explains Hukam further — Shabad LLL, Ang 1

This is particularly useful because the text immediately continues by repeatedly describing Hukam:

ਹੁਕਮੀ. ਹੋਵਨਿ ਆਕਾਰ; ਹੁਕਮੁ. ਨ ਕਹਿਆ ਜਾਈ ॥
ਹੁਕਮੀ. ਹੋਵਨਿ ਜੀਅ; ਹੁਕਮਿ. ਮਿਲੈ ਵਡਿਆਈ ॥
ਹੁਕਮੀ, ਉਤਮੁ ਨੀਚੁ; ਹੁਕਮਿ ਲਿਖਿ, ਦੁਖ ਸੁਖ ਪਾਈਅਹਿ ॥
ਇਕਨਾ. ਹੁਕਮੀ ਬਖਸੀਸ; ਇਕਿ, ਹੁਕਮੀ ਸਦਾ ਭਵਾਈਅਹਿ ॥

Source translation:

“By His Command, bodies are created; His Command cannot be described.”

“By His Command, souls come into being; by His Command, glory and greatness are obtained.”

“By His Command, some are high and some are low; by His Written Command, pain and pleasure are obtained.”

“Some, by His Command, are blessed and forgiven; others, by His Command, wander aimlessly forever.” sggs_multilingual.txt

Then:

ਹੁਕਮੈ ਅੰਦਰਿ. ਸਭੁ ਕੋ; ਬਾਹਰਿ ਹੁਕਮ. ਨ ਕੋਇ ॥
hukamai andar sabh ko; baahar hukam na koe

ਨਾਨਕ. ਹੁਕਮੈ ਜੇ ਬੁਝੈ; ਤ ਹਉਮੈ. ਕਹੈ ਨ ਕੋਇ ॥੨॥
naanak. hukamai je bujhai; ta haumai. kahai na koe

Source translation:

“Everyone is subject to His Command; no one is beyond His Command.”

“O Nanak, one who understands His Command, does not speak in ego.” sggs_multilingual.txt

My interpretation: This makes the idea substantially richer than “when something bad happens, accept that God wanted it.”

Hukam here appears to describe the order within which existence itself operates: creation, life, circumstances, pain and pleasure all occur within it. The human response is first ਚਲਣਾ (chalṇā), walking according to it, and then ਬੁਝੈ (bujhai), understanding it. The stated consequence of understanding Hukam is specifically the dissolution of ਹਉਮੈ (haumai, ego).

So “acceptance” is certainly compatible with the passage—especially accepting that reality isn’t under one’s personal control—but passivity isn’t stated.

3. Japji later reinforces the distinction — Shabad A8N, Ang 7

Later, Guru Nanak says:

ਆਖਣਿ ਜੋਰੁ; ਚੁਪੈ. ਨਹ ਜੋਰੁ ॥
ਜੋਰੁ ਨ. ਮੰਗਣਿ; ਦੇਣਿ. ਨ ਜੋਰੁ ॥
ਜੋਰੁ ਨ. ਜੀਵਣਿ; ਮਰਣਿ. ਨਹ ਜੋਰੁ ॥
…
ਜਿਸੁ ਹਥਿ ਜੋਰੁ; ਕਰਿ ਵੇਖੈ ਸੋਇ ॥
ਨਾਨਕ; ਉਤਮੁ ਨੀਚੁ ਨ ਕੋਇ ॥੩੩॥

Source translation:

“No power to speak, no power to keep silent.”

“No power to beg, no power to give.”

“No power to live, no power to die.”
…
“He alone has the Power in His Hands. He watches over all.”

“O Nanak, no one is high or low.” sggs_multilingual.txt

My interpretation: Notice that the examples themselves contain actions—speaking, remaining silent, begging, giving, living. The point isn’t “therefore don’t act.” It appears to undermine the ego’s claim of independent authorship/control over action.

That fits remarkably well with the earlier sequence:

act/walk → but recognize everything occurs within Hukam → understanding this undermines haumai.

So does hukam rajāī chalṇā mean “be passive”?

Based strictly on these passages, I don’t think the source supports that reading.

A more textually careful formulation would be:

Live and act within the recognition that reality is not governed by your ego or personal will.

That’s my interpretation, not the supplied English translation. The supplied translation itself is: “obey the Hukam of His Command, and walk in the Way of His Will.” sggs_multilingual.txt

And importantly, the problem Hukam solves in the verse isn’t “How do I stop caring what happens?” It is “How can I become truthful, and how can the veil of illusion be torn away?” The next pauri then explicitly connects understanding Hukam with haumai ceasing. sggs_multilingual.txt

So within the text alone, I’d characterize it less as fatalistic resignation and more as active living without the illusion that the individual ego is sovereign over reality.
